Agentic Transaction Architecture

When AI agents can act, architecture matters.

AI agents are moving beyond recommendations and analysis toward initiating and completing business transactions: purchasing, vendor coordination, subscription management, procurement. Horizon designs the operating environments that make these capabilities coordinated, accountable, secure, and useful.

The architecture challenge

When an AI agent can initiate a transaction, who authorizes it? Who verifies it? Who records it? And who remains accountable when something goes wrong? Tools alone do not answer those questions. Architecture does.

Conceptual architecture. It represents no particular vendor's product or payment system. The agent is one component; the value is in the relationships among all of them.

What it is

The design of an operating environment in which AI agents can initiate, coordinate, verify, and execute authorized business transactions. Horizon designs agent roles, transaction workflows, authority boundaries, human approvals, information architecture, verification, and exception handling. This is not an AI shopping assistant and not a payment product. It is architecture.

Who it is for

Organizations considering AI-enabled purchasing, procurement, vendor coordination, or recurring operational expenditures, and organizations already experimenting with agents that touch money and need the authority structure to catch up.

Governance and human authority

Every transaction environment Horizon designs specifies spending limits, approval requirements, identity verification, audit trails, and reconciliation, along with escalation paths for exceptions. Agents act within boundaries people set, and every action leaves a record a person can examine.

What you walk away with

Engagements begin with an Agentic Transaction Readiness Assessment: a transaction opportunity and risk inventory, an assessment of existing workflows and systems, an agent authority and human approval model, a preliminary transaction architecture, and phased implementation recommendations. Design and implementation support follow from there, separately scoped.

The technology will change. The architecture must endure.

The value of this work does not depend on any one vendor's agent framework or payment system. Technologies will be replaced. The authority structure, verification discipline, and records have to hold through the replacements.

What it costs to start

Agentic Transaction Readiness Assessments typically range from $2,500 to $5,000, depending on the number of transaction types and systems in scope. Deliverables and price are agreed in writing before work begins.

Horizon is an architectural consultancy. It does not process payments, hold funds, or certify legal compliance, and it coordinates with your security, legal, finance, and technology specialists where the design touches their domains.
